# Career Profile

## What this part is for

The Career Profile is the user's durable [[00 Foundations/Working Language#Career record|career record]]. It should hold far more context than a CV ever can: the details that are useful later but too specific, old, or situational to include in every application.

The user should be able to put down the whole story without having to decide immediately whether a detail is resume-worthy. currnt can help turn that material into useful evidence over time, but it must not flatten the user's career into generic summaries.

The profile is also a living picture of the present and future. [[Career Direction]] holds where the user is now, what they want next, and the professional direction they are trying to build toward.

## What belongs here

- Roles, employers, dates, locations, and changes in responsibility.
- Projects, programmes, products, initiatives, and side work.
- Responsibilities, decisions, outcomes, achievements, and failures that taught something important.
- Metrics, scale, budgets, teams, stakeholders, systems, markets, constraints, and context.
- Skills, tools, methods, domains, certifications, education, and languages.
- Source material such as rough notes, imported CVs, and supporting documents where the user wants to keep them.

The useful unit is not only "I held this role." It is "in this context, I did this work, with these constraints, and this was the result."

## How it should feel

- Like a calm, private professional archive rather than a profile-completion checklist.
- Spacious enough for long-form thinking, but structured enough to make details reusable.
- Respectful of unfinished memory. A rough note is valid input.
- Clear about what is source material, what is accepted fact, and what is a polished application claim.
- More like working with a thoughtful editor than filling in a recruiter form.

## Relationship to the rest of currnt

The profile is the evidence base for everything else.

- [[Job Tracking and Assessment|Job assessment]] should point back to the specific profile evidence that supports or fails to support its conclusions.
- [[Application Documents|CVs and cover letters]] should draw from it without changing it by accident.
- AI can suggest structure, missing detail, or stronger wording, but the user remains the authority on their experience.
- A document made for one job may reveal a valuable fact worth adding back to the profile, through an intentional action rather than an automatic write.
- Career Direction gives assessment and writing a forward-looking lens without changing the factual career record.

## The vault model

The profile should be a connected vault, not a collection of isolated form records. Users can link roles, projects, evidence, and skills in the context of their own writing. The product then uses those connections to make the record easier to navigate and retrieve from.

See [[Vault Graph and Links]] for the distinction between lightweight reference links and confirmed professional relationships.

## Important tensions to protect

- Richness should not become a burden to maintain.
- Structure should not force people to remember their career in one prescribed format.
- An AI-generated inference must not quietly become a stated fact.
- A polished CV is an output of the profile, not a replacement for it.

## Questions to keep open

- What makes it easy to capture a thought quickly, then return later to add context?
- How should the product distinguish an achievement from a responsibility without implying one is more valuable?
- When does a metric need a source or confidence note?
- Which raw materials should be retained, and which should remain outside the product?
