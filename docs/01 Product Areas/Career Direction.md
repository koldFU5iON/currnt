# Career Direction

## What this part is for

Career Direction makes the profile a living view of the user's career, not only an archive of what they have done.

It holds the user's current professional position, the work they want more or less of, and the direction they are actively trying to move toward. This gives [[Job Tracking and Assessment|job assessment]] a meaningful definition of fit beyond matching keywords or previous titles.

## What it can hold

- The user's current role, professional identity, and strongest current themes.
- Roles, seniority, responsibilities, and environments they want to move toward.
- Industries, domains, organisations, or types of work they are drawn to.
- Work they want to do more of, less of, begin doing, or leave behind.
- Practical constraints and preferences: location, remote expectations, salary needs, travel, team shape, or working style.
- Longer-term ambitions and the experiences they may need to build toward them.
- Questions and uncertainties the user is still working through.

This should allow nuance. A person may be open to more than one direction, may be testing a hypothesis, or may not yet have a crisp job title in mind.

## How it should feel

- Reflective, useful, and allowed to change.
- More like a career conversation than a recruitment preference form.
- Honest about trade-offs and uncertainty.
- Specific enough to guide decisions, without forcing false precision.

## Relationship to the career record

The [[Career Profile]] answers: "What have I done, and what evidence supports that?"

Career Direction answers: "Where am I now, and where am I trying to go?"

Together they form the user's career intelligence. The record gives direction credibility and context; direction gives the record a lens for selection and development.

## Relationship to job assessment

A role can be a strong match for past experience but a poor fit for the user's intended future. Conversely, it can be a calculated stretch that helps the user move toward a desired direction.

Assessment should therefore consider both:

- **Evidence fit:** Does the user's career record credibly support this role?
- **Direction fit:** Does this role move the user toward, away from, or sideways from their stated goals?

The product should make the trade-off visible rather than reducing it to one score.

## Relationship to application documents

Career Direction should inform positioning, not fabricate experience. It can help choose the story a CV leads with and the argument a cover letter makes, while every factual claim remains grounded in the career record.

## Questions to keep open

- How should the user update direction as their goals change without losing useful history?
- Should a person be able to maintain several possible directions at once?
- How do we make practical constraints useful without making the product feel restrictive?
- When does a role represent a healthy stretch rather than an unrealistic detour?
