# Job Tracking and Assessment

## What this part is for

This area helps the user bring an [[00 Foundations/Working Language#Opportunity|opportunity]] into currnt, understand it in context, and keep a clear record of what they chose to do next.

It is not job discovery for its own sake. A job may come from a job board, recruiter, referral, company site, message, or saved link. currnt's job begins when the user wants to make sense of it.

## Bringing in an opportunity

Adding a job should be quick enough to happen at the moment of discovery. A user should be able to capture a link from outside currnt, add minimal context, and return later for a proper review.

The product should preserve where the job came from. When possible, it should also help identify the original company posting or careers page. That is useful context, not a promise: some companies and job boards will not make a canonical source available.

The user should never lose control of the captured information. Extraction is a starting point to review, not a hidden transformation.

## The assessment

Assessment is a core decision tool, not a decorative score.

It should answer questions such as:

- Is this worth my time?
- What in my career directly supports this role?
- What is missing, uncertain, or potentially risky?
- Does this move me toward the work and [[Career Direction|direction]] I want?
- What story would I need to lead with if I pursue it?
- What compensation is stated, and if none is stated, what is a sensible estimated range?

### Quick pass

The quick pass is for triage. It should be concise, honest, and inexpensive to run. It gives the user a recommendation to skip, review, or pursue, along with the strongest reasons, obvious blockers, unknowns, and any clearly stated pay.

It should not pretend to be definitive when the job description is thin or the profile is incomplete.

### Deep dive

The deep dive is for opportunities the user is seriously considering. It should take the job seriously and show its work:

- Requirement-by-requirement fit and evidence.
- Meaningful gaps and ways to address them honestly.
- Career-path and positioning implications.
- Risks that may affect the application or interview process.
- Salary context and assumptions.
- Clear priorities for the CV and cover letter.

## How it should feel

- Deliberate rather than frantic.
- Direct but not discouraging.
- Helpful in making a decision, not designed to gamify a pipeline.
- Clear about facts, estimates, assumptions, and unknowns.
- More like a trusted career adviser preparing a briefing than a kanban board demanding updates.

## Relationship to the rest of currnt

- An opportunity is assessed against the [[Career Profile]].
- Assessment considers both past evidence and the user's stated Career Direction.
- The assessment should become the brief for [[Application Documents|application documents]].
- CVs and cover letters stay connected to the opportunity they were made for.
- Job status is a record of the user's real process, not the centre of the product.
- Salary understanding belongs alongside fit and risk, not in an isolated calculator.

## Boundaries

currnt can make capture and review easier. It should not try to submit applications, impersonate the user, promise that it can find every original job source, or become an autonomous job-hunting agent.
