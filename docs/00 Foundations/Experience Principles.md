# Experience Principles

## Calm is a feature

Job seeking and career decisions can already feel high pressure. currnt should reduce cognitive load rather than amplify it with streaks, noisy dashboards, urgency cues, or artificial productivity metrics.

## The user's history is the substance

The most valuable material in the product is the user's real career evidence. Interface and automation exist to make that material easier to understand and use. They should not compete with it.

## Show the work

An assessment, source resolution, salary estimate, imported fact, or AI suggestion should have visible grounding. Users should be able to inspect the inputs, reasoning, confidence, and limitations that matter to a decision.

## Preserve authorship

The user owns their career record and final application documents. AI may propose; the user decides. The product should make review, revision, and rejection easy.

## One connected story

Profile, opportunity, assessment, CV, cover letter, and export are stages in a single journey. A user should be able to move between them without re-entering context or wondering which version is current.

## Editorial, not administrative

The product should feel like a considered workspace for thinking and writing, not an HR system or generic form builder. Structure is there to support judgment, not to make the user perform data entry.

## Precision over novelty

Use visual emphasis, interaction, and automation only where they make a decision or document clearer. A quiet, reliable interaction is more valuable than a clever one that obscures the task.
